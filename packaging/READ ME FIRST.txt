UWUVCI for macOS
================

A native Mac version of UWUVCI AIO, the Wii U Virtual Console injector.
Nothing else needs to be installed: no Wine, no .NET, no helper program.


1. INSTALL

   Drag "UWUVCI" into your Applications folder (or anywhere you like).


2. FIRST START  (one time only)

   The app is not signed with a paid Apple developer certificate, so macOS
   asks you to approve it the first time:

     a. Double-click UWUVCI. macOS says it can't be opened. Click "Done".
     b. Open System Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click
        "Open Anyway" next to the message about UWUVCI.
     c. Confirm with "Open Anyway" (and your password or Touch ID).

   On macOS 14 and older you can instead right-click UWUVCI and choose
   "Open", then "Open" again.

   If you are comfortable with Terminal, this one line does the same:

     xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/UWUVCI.app

   After that it opens like any other app.


3. USING IT

   On first start UWUVCI downloads a few small files it needs.

   Pick a console on the left, choose a base game, choose your ROM, and
   press Inject. Everything works the same way as on Windows, so the
   regular guides apply:

     https://uwuvci-prime.github.io/UWUVCI-Resources/

   You need the Wii U Common Key and the Title Key of the base game you
   want to use, exactly as on Windows. UWUVCI checks them when you enter
   them but does not provide them. You can also use a base you dumped from
   your own Wii U ("Custom base").

   Finished injects are saved to  Documents/UWUVCI/InjectedGames
   (you can change this under Settings).


NOTES

   * Wii and GameCube injects need about 15 GB of free disk space while
     they are being made.
   * Config files (.uwuvci) are interchangeable with the Windows version.
   * Wii cheat-code (GCT) patching uses a tool that only exists for Intel
     Macs. On Apple Silicon it needs Rosetta; if it is not installed,
     run this in Terminal:  softwareupdate --install-rosetta
   * Not available on macOS, because they depend on closed Windows-only
     programs: Wii video mode patching (NTSC <-> PAL), NES/SNES "Pixel
     Perfect" mode, the cafe2wii (c2w) patcher, and the built-in
     Nintendont and N64 INI editors.
   * Your settings, downloaded bases and logs live in
     ~/Library/Application Support/UWUVCI
